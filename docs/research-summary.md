# Research summary: faster code review without losing quality

Background research for the module-level review tool. Every finding links to a source that was opened or found during research. Vendor-reported numbers are labelled as vendor numbers. Numbers that could not be checked against a primary source are flagged or left out.

## 1. Small PRs and stacked diffs

- **Google: small changes, one reviewer, fast turnaround.** Data from about 9M changes: the median change modifies 24 lines and about 90% touch fewer than 10 files. The median reviewer count is 1. First feedback arrives in under an hour for small changes and about 5 hours for very large ones, and the median for the whole review is under 4 hours. The authors link small changes directly to review quality. [Sadowski et al., ICSE-SEIP 2018](https://research.google/pubs/modern-code-review-a-case-study-at-google/)
- **Google's written guidance.** "100 lines is usually a reasonable size for a CL" and "1000 lines is usually too large." File spread also counts: 200 lines in one file can be fine, but the same 200 lines across 50 files is too large. Each CL should be one self-contained change, and reviewers may reject a CL just for its size. [Google eng-practices: Small CLs](https://google.github.io/eng-practices/review/developer/small-cls.html)
- **SmartBear/Cisco.** Review no more than 200–400 LOC at a time. Defect density drops sharply when reviewers go faster than about 500 LOC/hour, and reviewer performance falls off after about 60 minutes. These are vendor-published figures; the original study is behind a form and was not checked. [SmartBear best practices](https://smartbear.com/learn/code-review/best-practices-for-peer-code-review/)
- **Microsoft: usefulness drops with file count.** The more files in a review, the lower the share of useful comments, and the drop becomes noticeable at **20 or more changed files**. [Czerwonka, Greiler, Tilford 2015, "Code Reviews Do Not Find Bugs"](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/PID3556473.pdf)
- **Meta: tail latency matters more than averages.** Meta tracks P75 "Time In Review" because developer satisfaction follows the slowest 25% of diffs. It pairs this with "Eyeball Time" so that reviews are not rushed. Stacked diffs (Phabricator) are how Meta keeps each diff small. [Meta Engineering 2022](https://engineering.fb.com/2022/11/16/culture/meta-code-review-time-improving/), [Pragmatic Engineer on stacked diffs](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/stacked-diffs)
- **Graphite (vendor numbers, method not published).** Active users saw 65% less time between merged PRs and 16% less wait before first review. No independent controlled study of stacking was found. [Graphite docs](https://graphite.com/docs/why-you-should-use-graphite)

## 2. What reviewers actually catch: design vs. line level

- **Understanding is the main job; defects are a minority of the output.** Finding defects is still the top stated motivation, but reviews produce fewer defect findings than expected. They mostly produce knowledge transfer, team awareness, and alternative solutions. Reviewers spend most of their effort building context, and most of their strategies for doing so have no tool support. The authors recommend automating style checks so reviewers can focus on deeper problems. [Bacchelli & Bird, ICSE 2013](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=164195)
- **About 15% of comments flag a possible defect; at least 50% concern long-term maintainability.** Reviewers new to an area have only 33% of their comments judged useful, rising to about 67% by their third review of that area. [Czerwonka et al. 2015](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/PID3556473.pdf)
- **Most review findings are evolvability issues, not functional ones.** Roughly 75% of defects found in review affect evolvability or maintainability, not behavior. Secondary sources give 60–77%; the original paper was not checked. [Mäntylä & Lassenius, TSE 2009 (summary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_review), [arXiv 2411.11401 citing 77%](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.11401)
- **Google reviewers mainly cite education, norms, and gatekeeping.** Ownership-based approval puts extra scrutiny on changes outside the author's own code. [Sadowski et al. 2018](https://research.google/pubs/modern-code-review-a-case-study-at-google/)
- **What this means for the tool:** humans add the most value on design, structure, and context, which is the level a module/flow graph shows. Line-level correctness and style are better left to machines.

## 3. Module contracts and explicit interfaces

- **Information hiding (Parnas 1972).** Modules should hide design decisions that are likely to change. A good decomposition is one that contains change, not one that follows processing steps. [Parnas summary, The Morning Paper](https://blog.acolyer.org/2016/09/05/on-the-criteria-to-be-used-in-decomposing-systems-into-modules/)
- **Deep modules (Ousterhout).** A module's benefit is its functionality and its cost is its interface. A good module has a small interface over a large implementation, and a simple interface matters more than a simple implementation. [Book summary](https://freshman.tech/philosophy-of-software-design-summary)
- **Design by Contract (Meyer).** A contract has preconditions (the caller's obligations), postconditions (the supplier's guarantees), and invariants. A precondition violation is the caller's fault; a postcondition or invariant violation is the supplier's. Subtypes may weaken preconditions and strengthen postconditions, never the reverse. [DbC overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_by_contract), [Bern lecture notes](https://scg.unibe.ch/download/lectures/p2/P2-03-DesignByContract.pdf)
- **Consumer-driven contracts.** Consumers state which parts of a provider's contract they rely on, and the provider takes those statements on as obligations. The provider can then change everything else safely. [Ian Robinson, martinfowler.com 2006](https://martinfowler.com/articles/consumerDrivenContracts.html). Pact puts this into practice: a consumer test generates a pact file, which is then replayed against the provider. [Microsoft Engineering Playbook: CDC](https://microsoft.github.io/code-with-engineering-playbook/automated-testing/cdc-testing/)

## 4. Automated guardrails

- **Architecture fitness functions** give an objective, automated, continuous check of architectural characteristics, for example as unit tests or metrics. [Thoughtworks Radar](https://thoughtworks.com/radar/techniques/architectural-fitness-function)
- **ArchUnit (Java)** checks package and class dependencies, layers, slices, and cycles from bytecode inside ordinary unit tests. [archunit.org](https://www.archunit.org/). **dependency-cruiser (JS/TS)** enforces `forbidden` dependency rules (cycles, orphans, layer violations) and reports violations in an eslint-like format. [dependency-cruiser](https://github.com/sverweij/dependency-cruiser)
- **Static analysis only works if developers trust it.** Google targets less than 10% "effective false positives" (cases where developers take no action), shows results inside code review, and lets developers flag "Not useful". This criterion is reported second-hand; the CACM paper defines the term. [Sadowski et al., CACM 2018](https://cacm.acm.org/magazines/2018/4/226371-lessons-from-building-static-analysis-tools-at-google/pdf), [Sourcegraph notes](https://about.sourcegraph.com/blog/code-security-at-google-find-fix-vulnerabilities-in-code)
- **Mutation testing at review time.** Across more than 70,000 diffs, Google surfaced 150,000 actionable mutants in code review. A developer feedback loop raised their reported usefulness from about 20% to 80%. [State of Mutation Testing at Google](https://research.google/pubs/state-of-mutation-testing-at-google/)
- **Property-based testing** helps most with complex code. Its cost is writing generators and properties. Evidence is mostly qualitative (30 interviews at Jane Street). [Goldstein et al., ICSE 2024](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/property-based-testing-in-practice/)
- **CODEOWNERS with "Require review from Code Owners"** routes each change to the owning team automatically and blocks merge until an owner approves. [GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/about-code-owners)

## 5. C4, diagrams as code, and change-impact analysis

- **C4 model.** Hierarchical zoom levels: context, then containers, then components, then code. For component diagrams, C4 advises creating them only when they add value and "consider automating their creation" for long-lived documentation. [c4model.com](https://c4model.com/diagrams/component)
- **Diagrams as code.** Structurizr generates many views from one model, so diagram changes go through version control and review like code. [Structurizr](https://structurizr.com)
- **The model-code gap.** Hand-drawn diagrams drift away from the code. Simon Brown's fix is an "architecturally-evident coding style": when code makes its components explicit, tooling can scan for them and fill in the model automatically. [Simon Brown via DZone](https://dzone.com/articles/software-architecture-code)
- **Impact analysis at PR level.** Combining call-graph reachability with repository-history mining gives reviewers impact information inside the PR. [Empirical SE 2024](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10664-024-10600-2). Reachability over-approximates by design, so results need ranking. [SciTools](https://docs.scitools.com/help/compare/change-impact-analysis.html), [Chianti (IBM)](https://dominoweb.draco.res.ibm.com/reports/rc22983.pdf)

## 6. Reviewing AI- and agent-written code (2025–2026)

- **More duplication, less reuse.** GitClear analysed 211M changed lines (2020–2024). Cloned lines rose from 8.3% to 12.3%. Refactoring-related "moved" lines fell from 25% to under 10%. In 2024, copy-pasted lines outnumbered moved lines for the first time. [GitClear 2025](https://www.gitclear.com/ai_assistant_code_quality_2025_research)
- **Agents skip reuse, and reviewers do not notice.** Agent PRs contain more semantic (Type-4) redundancy than human PRs, yet reviewers react to them more positively. Plausible-looking code hides the debt, and existing clone detectors miss these clones. [Huang et al., MSR 2026, arXiv 2601.21276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21276v1)
- **Stability suffers.** DORA 2024 estimated a 7.2% drop in delivery stability (and 1.5% in throughput) as AI adoption rose, and hypothesised that larger batch sizes are the cause. [Google Cloud / DORA 2024](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/devops-sre/announcing-the-2024-dora-report). DORA 2025 calls AI an "amplifier": throughput is now positively correlated with AI use, but instability persists, and loosely coupled architectures benefit most. [InfoQ on DORA 2025](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/09/dora-state-of-ai-in-dev-2025)
- **Review becomes the bottleneck.** Faros telemetry (vendor, about 10k developers): high-AI teams merge about 98% more PRs, but review time rises 91%. PR-size growth is reported inconsistently (154% vs. 51%), so it is not used here. [Faros AI research](https://faros.ai/research)
- **Developers misjudge their own speed.** In METR's RCT (16 experienced OSS developers, 246 tasks), AI use made developers 19% slower, while they believed it had made them 20% faster. [METR via Simon Willison](https://feeds.simonwillison.net/2025/Jul/12/ai-open-source-productivity/)
- **Specific failure modes.** CodeRabbit (vendor, 470 PRs) found 1.7x more issues per AI PR (10.83 vs. 6.45), with logic errors about 75% more common. [CodeRabbit](https://coderabbit.ai/blog/state-of-ai-vs-human-code-generation-report). Veracode found models introduced flaws in 45% of 80 security-sensitive tasks. [Veracode 2025](https://www.veracode.com/press-release/ai-generated-code-poses-major-security-risks-in-nearly-half-of-all-development-tasks-veracode-research-reveals/). In one study, 19.7% of LLM-suggested packages did not exist ("slopsquatting"). [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2025/04/14/package-hallucination-slopsquatting-malicious-code/). GitHub's agent-PR guide lists: **CI gaming** (removed or skipped tests, `|| true`, lowered coverage thresholds; "any CI weakening is a hard stop"), **reuse blindness**, **hallucinated correctness** (trace one critical path and require a test that fails before the change), and large PRs with no plan. It suggests asking for a split when a PR touches more than 5 unrelated files or its purpose can't be stated in one sentence. [GitHub Blog 2026](https://github.blog/2026-04-08-agent-pull-requests-are-everywhere-heres-how-to-review-them/). Thoughtworks warns against complacency with AI-generated code. [Radar vol. 32](https://thoughtworks.com/content/dam/thoughtworks/documents/radar/2025/04/tr_technology_radar_vol_32_en.pdf)

## Recommendations for this tool

- **R1 – Module contract schema.** Each module declares: input and output types; preconditions and postconditions (DbC); side effects as typed entries (DB read/write with table and operation, outbox event, Kafka topic and schema, HTTP method and host/path, cache, file/IO); errors thrown; idempotency; and owner (CODEOWNERS). The contract is the review surface; the implementation is not.
- **R2 – Show contract diffs first.** Open the review on a list of contract changes sorted by risk (new or removed side effects > signature or schema changes > new dependency edges > implementation-only changes). Fully collapse modules whose contract did not change.
- **R3 – Undeclared side effects block the merge.** Static analysis compares actual DB, Kafka, HTTP, and outbox calls against the declared contract and fails CI on any mismatch. This removes the "hidden side effects" failure mode and keeps the graph trustworthy.
- **R4 – Size budget per review unit, counted in modules.** Default limits: at most about 5 modules with changed contracts, about 400 changed LOC, and fewer than 20 files. Above that, require splitting into a stack, with each stack layer shown as its own graph diff.
- **R5 – Impact view from changed modules up to entry points.** For each change, list every API, consumer, or cron entry point that reaches it. Rank by whether the change crosses a contract (contract change = real ripple; implementation-only = low) and by history, because plain reachability over-approximates.
- **R6 – Architecture fitness functions in CI.** Enforce allowed dependency directions, no cycles, no cross-module access to internals, and "only module X writes table T" rules (ArchUnit / dependency-cruiser style), and show violations as red edges in the graph.
- **R7 – A machine gate before any human sees the review.** Lint, typecheck, SAST, dependency/package-existence check, contract tests (Pact-style for Kafka/HTTP), and mutation testing on changed modules. Track each checker's false-positive rate and demote any checker above 10% effective false positives.
- **R8 – Flag AI-specific risk signals.** CI or test weakening (deleted or skipped tests, `|| true`, lowered thresholds) = hard stop. Also flag: new helpers that are near-duplicates of existing modules (semantic clone check); new external dependencies or unknown packages; new side effects that the PR description does not mention; and changes that touch many modules unrelated to the stated goal.
- **R9 – Require intent and evidence on every PR.** A one-sentence goal plus the list of expected contract changes. The tool diffs expected against actual changes. Each changed postcondition needs a test that fails on the old behavior.
- **R10 – Route reviews by ownership.** Auto-request the owner of every module whose contract changed. Track P75 time-in-review and reviewer dwell time so speed gains do not come from rubber-stamping.
- **R11 – Keep the graph generated from code.** Never hand-draw it. Use C4-style zoom levels (service, then flow, then module, then sub-module), and keep the model/contract files version-controlled and reviewed alongside the code so the diagram cannot drift.
- **R12 – Reserve human attention for design questions.** Show prompts in the reviewer UI ("Is this the right module for this side effect?", "Does this widen the interface?", "Could an existing module be reused?"). Measure review usefulness, not just throughput.
